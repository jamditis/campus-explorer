# Scripted builds

Parts of the campus built whole by script from plan files: one folder per area, one `.blend` for all of them, exported to the viewer as a third source next to the main and north models (`sw_` tiles). Every run rebuilds the whole file from the plan files and scripts here, so the modelling box can make the same objects from this checkout.

| `west/` | Carlisle Road north of the south-west area, its roadside parking (Lots 30 and 30S), Lot 33 and the College Avenue junction beside the Red Hawk Deck: a strip between the railway and the buildings of the main and north models |
| `village/` | West of the railway: Clove Road, Hawk Crossings, Sinatra Hall, The Village (Williams, Fenwick, Paul and Count Basie halls, the Abbott and Costello Center), Lots 27, 28, 29, 41, 45, 46, 47, 48 and 61 with its solar canopies |

| `stadium/` | Yogi Berra Stadium and the museum, the running track and soccer field, the north part of the softball stadium, the lots and roads north of the north-east area |
| `northeast/` | CarParc Diem, Dinallo Heights, Machuga Heights, the Student Recreation Center, the District Energy Plant, the softball stadium, the tennis courts, and the lots and roads between them |

The engine (`*.py` in this folder) is shared. An area is a folder with a `footprint.json`; object names carry the area or a zone name, and zone names are unique across areas.

Evidence order here: public photographs, then the NJ 2020 ortho (plan), then 3DEP LiDAR (heights), then OpenStreetMap. Joe's photos beat all of these when they exist. Nothing in this folder may be presented as measured unless it was: inferred sizes and positions carry `UNVERIFIED` in the object name and a note in the plan file.

## Run it

```sh
# 1. Evidence per area, into a folder outside the repository named as the area (/workspace/data/<area>)
python3 tools/evidence/fetch.py ortho southwest -560 -380 -200 -50 0.15
python3 tools/evidence/fetch.py lidar southwest -560 -380 -200 -50 0.5
python3 tools/evidence/fetch.py osm   southwest -560 -380 -200 -50

# 2. Paint sheets (system Python with Pillow), then the .blend (Blender 4.5)
#    for every area (or --areas NAME,NAME)
python3 blender/scripted/paint.py /workspace/data
blender -b --factory-startup --python blender/scripted/build.py -- /workspace/data OUT/montclair_southwest.blend

# 3. Tiles for the viewer (see tools/web-export/build_web.sh for the box layout it expects)
SCENE=sw bash tools/web-export/build_web.sh OUT/montclair_southwest.blend
```

`python3 -m unittest discover -s tests/py` checks the geometry helpers and every plan file. CI runs it.

`<area>/edge_heights.json` holds the height of the neighbouring models' ground along the footprint, so the new ground meets it flush. Make it again only when the footprint or the neighbours change. From a triangle dump of tiles `supersede.mjs` has cut (`edge.py` finds `tiles.json` in the folder) it is the top of the other sources' ground meshes 5 cm outside the footprint, every metre, where the two meshes meet, plus a sample either side of each step of more than 0.3 m between two samples (a skirt meets another model's sidewalk), found to 5 cm; a step at a corner of the footprint gets one sample 2.5 cm along each of its two edges instead of the corner's own. Read 0.3 m outside (sw16, from tiles whose cut could lie up to half a metre off), the north model's terrain, which falls up to 1 m per metre from its cut edge, came out up to 0.25 m low. Every area's file was made again this way in sw18 from a dump of the published tiles (sw17's for the areas the ponds do not touch; for `lot60`, `village`, `ponds` and `southwest`, of scratch copies of the fc1401b tiles cut as the next export cuts them), with one exception: near the points where a no-tree build with the new samples put the edge more than 0.08 m further from the far field than sw17 has it, the earlier file's samples stay (within 6 m, then 14 m where that was not enough: an edge sample's offset is averaged over 12 samples each side), so no point is worse than sw17 by more than 0.1 m against the far field it meets (the file's `source` says how many points). The samples the area's paving was reviewed with stay in the file under `paving` (`seam.paving_samples`, `Terrain(paving_edge=...)`): paving within 0.15 m of the far field bends to those, so remaking the samples moves soft ground only. Walks marked `"grade": false` follow the ground and move with it. The first areas were first made from a 1 m plan raster of tiles that still had their ground inside, the median of both sides of the outline (inside it is the ground being replaced, which near a coarse skirt can lie a metre off); those samples are what stays in the exceptions above:

```sh
node tools/evidence/dump_tiles.mjs /workspace/data/normal/edge -280 -560 -20 -260 models/main --no-instances
python3 blender/scripted/edge.py normal /workspace/data/normal/edge
# the first way (before supersede.mjs ran on the tiles):
node tools/evidence/dump_tiles.mjs /workspace/data/southwest/tiles -560 -380 -200 -50 models/main
python3 tools/evidence/plan.py /workspace/data/southwest/tiles 1.0 --name ground --only "(?i)terrain|ground|skirt|farfield|lawn|asphalt|patch|apron|carriageway|walk|turf|fill|platform|ballast" --skip "(?i)far_houses|crown|Tree|roof|bleacher|field_lines|endzone"
```

`dump_tiles.mjs` imports packages, so run it from a folder whose `node_modules` holds `@gltf-transform/core`, `@gltf-transform/extensions` and `meshoptimizer` (ES modules do not read `NODE_PATH`): a copy of the script beside a link to `/workspace/nj-scenes/web/tools/node_modules` works.

## How it joins the other models

- **Two scripted areas side by side.** Along an edge that two areas share there is no other model to meet: edge samples there are dropped (`seam.far_samples`: a sample on or inside another area's outline goes; for edge heights read just outside from tiles, `edge.py`'s second way, the far-field samples beside a three-way corner stay, so the ground there meets the far field: sw16 dropped every sample within 2 m of another area, and Lot 60 stood 0.40 m over the far field at its corner with the Village; heights made the first way keep that 2 m rule, because next to another area they read that area's old ground), both areas take the ground from the same LiDAR, and paving is graded across the edge as one surface. Each area densifies its own outline, so the two meshes would put their vertices on the shared edge at different points: each area's outline also takes the other's points there (`seam.py`), and the vertices on it take the heights the area built first gave them (areas build in name order), so the two meshes meet on the same vertices at the same heights (measured between `descent` and `stadium`: 0.001 m median on paving and lawn). The outline points are not all the vertices a mesh puts there: a corner of one of its surfaces on the outline, or a point of `seam.ends`, is a vertex of one mesh only, and the other mesh's edge ran straight past it (sw17: 0.28 m between the north-east and stadium areas at (-66, 826), 0.40 m between the south-west and west areas at (-404.6, -57.5)). So every area records its mesh's outline vertices and edges (`seam.Seam`), a later area puts the recorded vertices that lie on its outline into it (`Seam.on_ring`), and a vertex of its own there that meets no recorded one takes the height of the recorded edge it lies on (`Seam.along`): the later mesh's edge lies on the first one's. Lot paving is only raised: a vertex of graded paving that is not a road, which only one of the two meshes had before (found by a second triangulation without the vertices taken from the other area), keeps its own height where the shared one is lower (`seam.taken`; taking it lowered Lot W of the stadium area by 0.07 m and the west strip's Carlisle Road by 0.33 m), so such a lot can still stand over the first area's edge (south-west/west: 0.36 m). Both areas' cuts of the far field on the same line left a sliver of it standing between them in the tiles (the far field stood up to 0.9 m over the new ground along `descent`'s edges with `stadium`, up to 3.6 m along the north-east and Webster areas' edge; 90th percentile 0.5 to 2.5 m on five pairs in sw16): an area whose `supersede` has `"overlap": true` cuts `OVERLAP` (0.6 m) into its neighbour along every stretch it shares (`supersede.grow_shared`: a run is split where a corner of the other outline lies on it, and a stretch of 1 m or more moves). 0.6 m is more than the far field's cut edge can lie inside the outline that cut it (a grid diagonal of the north model's far field, 0.55 m, below), so the neighbour's cut takes that edge away. Where a moved stretch meets another run, the corner is where their lines meet if the new outline there lies in this area or another; where the neighbour ends at a corner and ground no area owns lies past it, the moved stretch steps back to the old corner, as far along as it must to take none of it (sw16's opt-in by line corners alone took far field at corners in the Village, Lot 60, Webster, the west strip and the north-east area: holes in the ground). The tests check, for every area that asks for it, that the grown outline holds the old one, lies in this area or another, does not cross itself, and that every stretch two areas share is cut past by one of them. Every area that shares an edge asks for it, except `southwest`: the tiles that run6 restores from fc1401b carry `southwest`'s marks from an earlier version of these rules, so its rules must stay as they are (the west strip's outline reaches into it instead; its far field is cut exactly all the same, see below). Fetch each area's LiDAR at least 40 m beyond its footprint, so the ground near a shared edge is not cut off. Measured between the north-east and stadium areas: median difference 3 cm; where paving on one side meets soft ground on the other, up to a few decimetres (the edge skirt closes the gap).

- **A new area beside reviewed ones.** Sharing an edge drops the neighbour's edge samples there, so its soft ground lets go of the old far field and follows the LiDAR, and its paving would too. An area added later can set `"hold_neighbour_paving": true` in its `footprint.json` (with `hold_neighbour_paving_why`): the neighbours' paving then keeps the samples the new edge took away (`seam.held_samples`, `Terrain(paving_edge=...)`), so lots are not lowered and reviewed paving does not move; their soft ground and everything else follow today's rule. Set by `ponds` (Lot 60's L6S_paving would have moved +0.079 / -0.076 m, Lot 45's asphalt +0.049 m) and `blanton`.
- **An area added beside published ones.** Every area that asks for `overlap` grows into every neighbour, so a new neighbour changes its grown outline and its rules' hash, and `supersede.mjs` then refuses every tile it cut until the main and north tiles are exported again. A new area sets `"neighbours_keep_cuts": true` in its `supersede` rules (with `overlap`): the other areas do not grow into it (`supersede.grow_into`), so their rules and the marks their cuts left in the published tiles stay what they were, and this area alone cuts `OVERLAP` past the edges it shares with them. The tests check that the neighbours' rules are the same with the new area as without it. Set by `tvtower`.
- **Build order.** Areas build in name order, and on a shared edge the area built first sets the heights of the vertices both meshes have. An area fitted in among reviewed ones lists them in `"build_after"` (with `build_after_why`) in its `footprint.json`: it builds after them (`layout.build_order`) and takes their edge as it is, instead of moving their paving to its own heights. Set by `blanton`, which webster and the north-east area close in on every side.
- **Notches in a shared edge.** A shared edge with runs shorter than `MIN_SHARED` (an outline that runs round a door or a building's corner) stays put there while the runs either side move `OVERLAP` out; the grown outline then crossed itself (webster round Blanton Hall's east corner). The small loop is cut off at the crossing (`supersede._unloop`); no other area's grown outline changed.
- **One ground.** Inside the footprint (`<area>/footprint.json`) an area owns the ground. `tools/web-export/supersede.mjs` takes the other sources' ground out of their tiles there: it removes the triangles inside and cuts every triangle the outline crosses (a far-field triangle 100 m across included) exactly at the outline, so the old ground ends at the edge at its own height, reaching a quantisation step (2 to 6 cm) under the new ground. A cut corner is rounded to its mesh's quantisation grid; the far field's grids are coarse (6 cm in the 4 km skirt, 0.38 m in the north model's 12 km `BD_farfield_3DEP`), and rounded to the nearest point its cut edge ended up to a quarter metre short of the outline, a crack that showed the skirt 0.4 to 0.65 m below (`descent`'s south-east corner). On the far field (a grid of 5 cm or more: the skirt, `BD_farfield_3DEP`) the pieces the outline cut go to a primitive of their own per mesh and material with float positions, cut on the outline (5 mm under the new ground), on edges whose midpoints keep the grid the rest of the far field shares; their corners keep the height sw16 stored there (the edge heights of `descent`, `lot60` and `normal` were read from those tiles). Snapped to the grid instead (sw17's first export), the far field reached up to 0.55 m under the new ground and stood up to 0.4 m over it in shards (181 m² of far field more than 2 cm over the scripted ground inside the outlines in sw16, 16 in sw17, all of it `southwest`'s far field in the tiles restored from fc1401b, which sw17 still cut on the grid). A primitive an earlier version of the rules cut (`southwest`'s marks in those tiles: straddlers kept whole, their inside corners lowered by `tuck`) is mended, its lowered corners raised back, and then cut exactly like the rest (0.01 m² predicted for `southwest` on scratch copies of the fc1401b tiles). Its rules, and so its marks, stay as they are, so the export needs no other tiles. Some corners the earlier version lowered lie on the outline and read as just outside it now (a row of skirt corners 0.48 m low at (-421.8, -360.6)): on the far field a corner within 0.1 m outside the outline that stands 0.3 to 0.7 m (`tuck` give or take 40 %) under the highest corner it shares a triangle with further out is raised back too. On a grid of 1 to 5 cm a corner on the outline is stored at the nearest grid point at least 1 cm inside it (`cut.mjs` `snapQuantized`, `behind`). A far-field triangle whose corners all lie inside but which a notch of the outline crosses is cut too (sw16 removed it whole: holes down to nothing along 3,800 points of the outlines, most in `core`, the Village and Webster). It reads every tile, because a far-field ground filed under one tile reaches far past it. The new ground's skirt and bank face close the step between the two. The rules (which meshes are ground, which things are replaced) are in `<area>/footprint.json` under `supersede`.
- **Changing the footprint.** Removed triangles cannot be put back. If `<area>/footprint.json` changes, export the main and north tiles again (or restore them from before the scripted build) before `SCENE=sw`; `supersede.mjs` stops with a message if a tile was done with other rules.
- **What stays.** The north model's station (tracks, ballast, platforms, shelter, catenary, fences, signs) and its retaining wall at the field's north-east corner stay as they are. The main model's paved corner south-east of the field (drive mouth, dumpster enclosure, shrubs) stays too: the footprint runs round it.
- **What is replaced.** The main model's partial Sprague Field (ws167 to ws189: south-east quarter of the turf, lines, end zone blocks, a goalpost, the south part of the east bleachers, one light pole). Its lines lie about 14 degrees off the field in the 2020 ortho; this build fits the whole field to the ortho (0.15 m corner misfit) and draws the 2025 markings.
- **Paving is graded, soft ground is not.** A road is a built surface: its height is the LiDAR ground smoothed over 5 m using paved cells only, so a bank beside it does not pull it up. Soft ground bends to meet the neighbour's ground along the footprint over 4 m, to `EDGE_TUCK` (2 cm) under it, so the far field covers its edge (sw19, met flush, showed the skirt as a thin dark line wherever the edge stood a centimetre or more over the far field; tile heights are stored to about 1 cm); paving, graded or not, meets it flush, and the tuck fades out within 1 m of an edge shared with another scripted area, whose mesh takes these vertices as they are; paving meets it where the two are already within 0.15 m, and at a road cut (below). Where the neighbour stands higher, a bank face closes the step. A surface with `"grade": false` (a pad on a bank) follows the raw ground.
- **Road cuts (opt-in).** Only paving a plan marks `"road": true` ramps to a neighbour. Where such a road crosses the footprint (road 1 m in, and on 60 % of the first 8 m in, along the edge's normal or along a line up to 40 degrees off it, so a walk that crosses at a slant counts; a road along the edge does not) and the LiDAR runs on across the edge with a step under 0.25 m (read 2 to 6 m each side), a step to the neighbour is the neighbour's error: a coarse skirt or far-field ground that lies off the road beyond the cut. There the road, up or down, and the soft ground beside it ramp to the neighbour's height at the edge, the step eased out over the step divided by 0.06, from 4 m to at most 30 m; the share that meets and the ramp's length are spread over 4 samples each side, so the ramp has no corner along the edge. Where the LiDAR shows the step (a wall, a bank) or does not reach past the edge, the road keeps its grade. Paving without the flag keeps the rule above. Flag a road only after looking at what the ramp does: it bends LiDAR-true paving to hide a neighbour's error. Flagged so far: `normal` (Valley Road, its sidewalks, the drives to Normal Avenue); its cuts across Valley Road went from 1.2 to 1.4 m steps to 0.03 m (median).
- **Edge samples.** Offsets from the LiDAR (a neighbour that follows it) are averaged over 12 samples each side, heights (a coarse ground) over 3: a coarse ground is smooth already and keeps its bend round a corner. A run ends where the neighbour's height jumps by more than 0.3 m from one sample to the next (two meshes meet there). Where `edge_heights.json` places such a step to within 0.2 m (the samples either side of it, which `edge.py` adds), the ground mesh takes a vertex at both samples and a line of vertices from each straight in, square to the pair, as deep as the edge blend reaches (`Terrain.break_points`, `Terrain.break_lines`): each side of the step then takes only its own heights, and no triangle spans the step, at the edge or inside it. A rule tried after sw13 (break a run only at a step that holds over 3 samples, read whether a neighbour follows the LiDAR over the whole run) was measured against the published neighbours with the build's own triangulation and taken out again: it made 66 to 280 edge points more than 0.1 m worse than sw13.
- **Trees.** Trees of the other models are not touched. Far-field scatter trees and far-field houses inside the footprint are removed.
- **Track level.** The north model's track and platforms stand about 0.65 m above the LiDAR ground. `southwest/station.json` carries a `lifts` entry that raises this build's ground to them along the corridor, eased out over 10 m, so the road meets the rails at the crossing. Remove it when the north track is lowered. Things that stand on a platform carry `base`, the platform top read from the published tiles.

Where the footprint runs under another model's building (to take all the ground up to its wall), `footprint.json` may list `free_edges`: `[{"ring": [...], "why": "..."}]`. Edge samples inside such a ring are ignored, because the other model's ground there is hidden inside the building and may be far off the LiDAR; the edge then follows this area's own grade. Give the reason every time.

### A building rebuilt on another model's site: no ground, volumes

An area may own no ground at all: a building the main model already has, rebuilt here (`ccom/`). Its `footprint.json` then has no `footprint`, only `supersede.volumes`, and the build makes no ground mesh: building parts, points and fittings stand on the raw LiDAR ground of `DATA/<area>/lidar.npz` (no edge blend, no grading; the build stops when that file is missing and the area has building parts).

The volumes say what of the other sources' tiles (main, north, context, skirt, far houses) the area replaces. `supersede.mjs` applies them in order:

```json
"supersede": {"volumes": [
  {"name": "inside", "ring": [[x, y], ...], "drop": "all", "why": "the building"},
  {"name": "band", "ring": [[x, y], ...], "drop": "all", "keep": ["(?i)terrain|lawn|walk|shrub"], "why": "eaves, gutters"},
  {"name": "shared_north", "ring": [[x, y], ...], "drop": "all", "above": 146.8, "why": "over the neighbour's roof"},
  {"name": "sign", "meshes": ["SCMSIGN"], "drop": "all", "why": "old lettering"}
]}
```

- A `ring` volume takes out every triangle inside the ring, at any height, or with `above` (NAVD88 metres) only what lies above that level. A triangle that a wall of the ring or the level crosses is cut exactly there (`tools/web-export/volume.mjs`, on `cut.mjs`), so a wall or an eave that runs on into a neighbouring building ends clean on the ring. `keep` patterns (on "mesh name material name"; a leading `(?i)` ignores case) leave whole primitives alone. Instanced nodes (shrubs, repeated fittings) lose the instances whose origin lies inside, unless their mesh or material matches `keep`. Far-field trees inside a ring with no `keep` and no `above` go too.
- A `meshes` volume drops meshes of exactly those names wherever they are. Mind that the exporter joins meshes by material, so a tile's mesh can carry more than its name says.
- `supersede.py` refuses a ring that crosses itself or has a corner sharper than 10 degrees (the miter spike an offset ring gets at the end of a shared wall).
- Marks work as for footprints: a done primitive, node and tile scene carry the area's hash (of all its volumes), a second run changes nothing, and a changed volume stops the run until the tiles are exported again.
- Walls shared with a neighbour: the tiles join this building's and the neighbour's eave courses into one mesh (same material, same heights), so neither a ring nor a pattern can part them. A band past the shared wall with `above` set just over the neighbour's roof at the band's outer edge takes this building's eave and leaves the neighbour's roof whole; set the level from a section of the published tiles, not from the LiDAR roof top. Read `supersede.mjs`'s output (`VOLUME_STATS=FILE` writes what each volume took, per mesh and material) and look at a section after the first export.

### Adopting objects of the main model

An area's `adopt.json` takes hand-made objects of a main-model release into this build (`adopt.py`), so they are exported in `sw_` tiles while the volumes take the main model's copies out of its tiles:

```json
{"zone": "ccom_adopt", "notes": "...",
 "adopt": {"file": "montclair_state_20261118i.blend", "sha256": "<64 hex>",
  "groups": [
   {"name": "as_is", "objects": ["exact object name", ...]},
   {"name": "atrium", "objects": [...], "transform": {"rotate_z_deg": -13.7, "about": [0, 0], "translate": [0.15, -2.764, 0]}},
   {"name": "sign", "objects": [...], "text": {"Campus sign lettering": "COLLEGE OF\nCOMMUNICATION AND MEDIA"}}
  ]}}
```

- The file is found with `--adopt-from PATH` (the file or its folder), else beside `--assets`; a wrong sha256 or a missing object stops the build. Objects hidden in the release (render or viewport), and objects whose names the web export skips everywhere (`tools/web-export/export_skip.py`: GN instancers, task chairs, people placeholders), are not adopted.
- Only the listed objects are linked into the build, with what they need (meshes, materials and their packed images, node groups, modifier targets); data nothing uses afterwards is purged.
- Objects keep their modifiers, instancing (collection instances, geometry nodes, linked duplicates), custom properties and material names, so the export applies the same modifiers and gives the materials the same categories (`cat`) and far-version treatment as in the main tiles. They are placed by the main export's own rule (bounding-box centre, 160 m tiles) in `sw_t_*` tiles.
- A group `transform` turns the objects about the vertical through `about` and then moves them, in world space. Parents are cleared with the world transform kept, so a parent and its child are each moved once. A moved object whose modifiers read another object (a boolean cutter, a geometry-nodes object input) is made a plain mesh first, since the other object stays where it was.
- `text` sets the body of a text object; its font, size, extrude and material stay, and the export converts it as the main export does.
- `clip` (`[{"ring", "keep": "outside" | "inside"}]`, after the transform) deletes the faces whose world centroid lies on the other side; an object with modifiers is made a plain mesh first, a collection instance stays or goes by its origin, an object left with nothing is dropped. `swap_instances` (`{"collection": "other collection"}`) makes the group's collection instances show another collection of the release file. `place` (`[{"collection", "name", "matrix"}]`, 16 numbers row-major, a world matrix the group transform does not touch) adds new instances of a release collection. `materials` (`{"material": {"name", "colour": "#rrggbb"}}`) renames a material and gives it a flat colour; a name `SW_<kind>_...` fixes its category as for the scripted materials.
- `fix` (`"module:function"`, a module under `blender/`) runs a correction script on the group after its objects are appended, moved, clipped and recoloured: `function(objects, collection)` gets a dict of source name to adopted object and the collection they are linked into, edits them in place (mesh edits, material slots) and returns a list of new objects it made (or nothing). New objects are linked, named by the `SW_adopt_` rule and exported like the rest. An unknown module or function, a fix that raises, or a return that is not objects stops the build, naming the group.
- Names become `SW_adopt_<source name>`; a name over Blender's 63 bytes is cut and ends with a short hash of the source name (and `_UNVERIFIED` when the source carried that label). `adopt_source`, `adopt_group` and `adopt_file` custom properties name where each object came from.

## When several people digitise one area

Zones digitised at the same time do not see each other. Before the first build, check them against each other: lots that do not reach road asphalt at their mouths, stall rows that fall on lawn or on an island, paint under a walk, a walk lying along a carriageway, an island of one zone over another zone's road, items more than 1 m past the outline, walk ends inside buildings. In the Village a first pass found all of these; a second pass after the fixes found only small leftovers. Where a row and an island disagree, read the ortho again before flipping a side: twice the island's outline was wrong, not the row.

## Frame

Model metres, the same frame as every other source: +X east, +Y grid north, Z NAVD88 metres. Angles are degrees anticlockwise from east. The viewer's Z is minus this Y.

| `terrain.py` | Ground heights from the LiDAR (numpy only): soft ground follows it, paving is graded, lifts, the edge blend |
| `ground.py` | One seamless ground mesh from the plan: terrain, paving, turf, curbs |
| `paint.py` | Draws the marking textures (lines, stalls, field markings) from the plan. System Python with Pillow |
| `layout.py` | Loads the plan files: dense outlines, layers, which surface gets which paint sheet. No bpy |
| `marks.py` | Shapes of arrows, crosswalks and pavement words as paint outlines. No bpy |
| `stand.py` | Grandstand, press box and low bleacher builders |
| `fixtures.py` | Light poles, goalposts, scoreboard, fences, crossing gates, lamps, benches and the like |
| `massing.py` | Pure geometry of building parts: gable, hip, shed and mansard roofs, window bays. No bpy |
| `building.py` | Buildings from measured parts: walls, roofs, window rows, parking deck levels |
| `place.py` | Puts stands, fences, hedges, fixtures, trees and buildings where the plan says |
| `edge.py` | Samples the neighbours' ground along the footprint into `<area>/edge_heights.json` |
| `supersede.py` | Writes the rules for `tools/web-export/supersede.mjs` from `<area>/footprint.json`: footprint and patterns (grown into a neighbour along a shared edge with `overlap`), or volumes |
| `seam.py` | Shared edges between areas of one build: the other area's outline points, and the heights the first-built area gave them. numpy only |
| `adopt.py` | Appends, moves and links the objects an area's `adopt.json` takes from a main-model release |
| `rooms.py` | Pure interior geometry: wall runs with door openings as boxes, cut out of `keep_out` rings and kept inside the shell; prisms; room labels. No bpy |
| `interior.py` | Builds a plan's `interiors`: partitions, glazed partitions, slabs, stair and lift blocks, piers |
| `rough.py` | Pure. Rough interiors: a plan's `rough` entries made into `interiors` floors from a footprint, floor levels and the little public evidence there is (lining, lobby, corridors, cores, rooms, toilets only from evidence). No bpy |
| `build.py` | Entry point: `blender -b --python blender/scripted/build.py -- DATA_DIR OUT.blend [--assets MAIN.blend] [--adopt-from RELEASE.blend]` |

Evidence is fetched with `tools/evidence/fetch.py` into a data folder outside the repository. `tools/evidence/overlay.py` draws it with a metre grid and draws plan files over it.

## Plan files

```json
{
  "zone": "station",
  "notes": "what was read from which source, and what is a guess",
  "surfaces": [
    {"name": "Station_lot", "kind": "asphalt", "ring": [[x, y], [x, y, r], ...],
     "evidence": "ortho2020", "unverified": "south end hidden by trees"}
  ],
  "strips": [
    {"name": "Normal_Ave_N_walk", "kind": "concrete", "centre": [[x, y], ...], "width": 1.5, "evidence": "ortho2020"}
  ],
  "curbs": [
    {"name": "Normal_Ave_S_curb", "path": [[x, y], ...], "closed": false, "evidence": "ortho2020"}
  ],
  "markings": [
    {"type": "line", "path": [[x, y], ...], "width": 0.1, "colour": "yellow", "dash": [3.05, 9.15]},
    {"type": "area", "ring": [[x, y], ...], "colour": "white"},
    {"type": "stalls", "line": [[x, y], [x, y]], "count": 12, "depth": 5.5, "side": "left", "angle": 90, "colour": "white"},
    {"type": "arrow", "at": [x, y], "angle": 90, "shape": "straight_left", "colour": "white"},
    {"type": "crosswalk", "line": [[x, y], [x, y]], "width": 3.0, "style": "continental", "colour": "white"},
    {"type": "text", "at": [x, y], "angle": 90, "text": "STOP", "height": 2.4, "colour": "white"}
  ],
  "points": [
    {"name": "Lot_lamp_1", "kind": "lamp", "at": [x, y], "height": 7.5, "evidence": "lidar2017", "unverified": "head type"}
  ]
}
```

- A **path** is a list of `[x, y]` or `[x, y, r]`. `r` rounds that corner with a true arc of radius `r` metres, tangent to both neighbouring runs. Curves are never given as many short segments: use few vertices with radii, so every edge is a straight run or a true arc.
- **surfaces** are closed paths. `kind` is one of `asphalt`, `concrete`, `brick`, `turf`, `lawn`, `mulch`, `gravel`, `water`. Where surfaces overlap, the higher layer wins: lawn 0, mulch 1, gravel 2, water 2.5, asphalt 3, turf 4, concrete 5, brick 6. An optional `"layer"` number overrides the kind's layer. Neighbouring surfaces should overlap by a little (0.2 m) instead of leaving a gap; the layer rule then makes one shared edge. `"road": true` (on a surface or a strip) marks a public road's carriageway, its sidewalks or a drive that may ramp, up or down, to a coarse neighbour where it crosses the footprint (see "Road cuts (opt-in)"); other paving never does.
- Paint goes on `asphalt`, `turf` and `gravel` (a clay infield or warning track: painted gravel is drawn in a clay tone). A `lawn` surface takes paint only with `"paint": true` (a mown ball field with chalk lines); it is then shown as even, untinted grass.
- A `water` surface is level: one height for the whole ring, the 10th percentile of the LiDAR ground along its traced shore (where the trace lies on water the return is the water surface; a median would climb the bank), or an explicit `"level"`. The ground round it runs down to it in the same mesh. Water takes no paint and no trees. A tree that stands in a pond stands on a hole in the water (`holes`, a hummock of soft ground round it): `tools/evidence/trees.py` keeps trees in holes and calls none of them a conifer (the water under a leaf-off crown reads green in the ortho).
- **strips** are open centre paths with a `width` (walks, bands). They become surfaces.
- **curbs** run along the edge between a carriageway and what is behind it, on the carriageway side's edge line. Give them in any direction.
- **markings** are paint. They are drawn into the texture of the surface under them and never become geometry. `stalls`: `count` stalls along `line` give `count + 1` divider lines, each `depth` long, to the `side` ("left" or "right") of the direction from the first point to the second. `arrow`: a lane-use arrow pointing toward `angle`, `at` the middle of its length on the line of its shaft; `shape` is `straight`, `left`, `right`, `straight_left` or `straight_right`. Sizes are those of the FHWA Standard Highway Signs pavement-marking sheets (through arrow 3.0 m, turn arrow 2.5 m, through-and-turn 4.0 m; see `marks.py`). An optional `length` scales the whole shape (the small arrows of a parking aisle, when measured). `crosswalk`: `line` is the crossing's centre line across the road, edge to edge, and `width` its width along the road; `style` `continental` (the default) gives bars 0.6 m wide at 1.2 m centres parallel to the traffic (`bar` and `spacing` change them), `lines` two 0.3 m transverse lines on its edges, `ladder` both. `text`: a pavement word or a lane number, centred at `at` and read by someone facing `angle` (the direction of travel), `height` metres tall (default 2.4 m, 8 ft; the MUTCD asks for 6 ft or more) and `width` across (default from the letter count). The height and the narrow letters follow the standard; the letter shapes are the drawing library's plain sans stretched to size, not the FHWA pavement alphabet. Give a two-word message as two `text` markings, the first word nearest the driver. A stop bar is a `line` 0.3 to 0.6 m wide, or an `area`. A flat-roofed building part (the top level of a parking deck), or a deck's ramp (`roof` `shed`, `style` `deck`), may carry `markings` of its own, in model coordinates like the others: they are drawn into one sheet over that roof (`authored_<area>_d<part>_0`) and mapped straight down onto its roof deck or ramp.
- **lifts** (`ring`, `dz`, `feather`) raise the ground inside a ring by `dz`, eased to nothing over `feather` metres outside it. Use one only to meet another model's level, and say why in `unverified`. A negative `dz` lowers soft ground or a walk to another model's object that stands lower than the LiDAR (`blanton`: the main model's west entrance landings of Blanton Hall, 0.17 and 0.23 m); never lot paving. Where the other model's object must not lie under the ground, paving (asphalt, concrete, brick) may take a `level` (metres, NAVD88) as water does: every vertex of it is held there, on an edge shared with an area built before as well (`seam.taken` `keep`: that area's edge skirt closes the step). `blanton` holds the main model's three west landing tops so, 1 to 3 cm under them, beside a webster walk and plaza up to 0.29 m higher.
- **points** place objects: `lamp`, `light_pole`, `sign`, `gate`, `flasher`, `goalpost`, `scoreboard`, `bench`, `bike_rack`, `ticket_machine`, `mini_high`, `utility_pole`, `pylon` (lattice transmission tower; `angle` is the line direction), `mast` (self-supporting lattice mast: `height` of the body, `base_width` and `top_width` from the LiDAR, `antenna` above it, `angle` of a face; painted in seven bands of aviation orange and white), `foul_pole` (`angle` along the foul line away from home, `side` of fair ground), `soccer_goal` (`at` the middle of the goal line, `angle` toward the pitch), `scoreboard` (`width`, `board_height`, `clear` under it), `tree`. `height` in metres when the evidence gives one. A `bench` 4 m or longer is a team bench: `angle` is its long direction and it faces the turf surface whose middle is nearest (or `facing`); in a zone named `field` with a `field_frame`, benches are set along the sideline of that field.
- **stands** (`ring`, `front` edge, `rows`, `sections`) are aluminium bleachers on an open frame. A stand with `"style": "bowl"` is a solid stepped concrete bowl instead, with absolute levels (NAVD88 metres): `walk` (`[depth, level]` of the front walkway), `tiers` (`rows`, `first` row level, `rise`), one of `aisles` (`width`, `level`) between each pair of tiers, `back` (the level behind the top row, flush with a concourse), `base` (the block's bottom, below the ground in front), `tread` (default 0.85 m) and `seats` (`rows` `[first, last]` numbered through all tiers, `type` `chairs` with a `colour` or `bench`, optional `sections`); `aisle_width` stair aisles are gaps in the seat rows. Seats are one low strip per row and section, not single chairs. Inside a bowl's outline, and on it, the ground is held 3 cm under the bowl's top (on a riser, under the lower step), and the ground mesh has edges on the outline and on every riser, so no ground triangle spans a step; outside the outline nothing is lowered. **fences** (`path`, `height`, `kind`: `chain_link_black`, `netting_black`, `hedge`, or a solid wall: `wall_padded` (a ballpark's outfield wall), `wall_brick`, `wall_concrete`, with optional `colour` and `thickness`) and **buildings** are extra lists a zone may carry. `field_frame` (`centre`, `angle`) fixes the football field; its lines are generated from the rule book, never traced.
- **buildings** are lists of parts; split a building where the roof height or type changes. A part has a `ring`, a `roof` (`flat`, `gable`, `hip`, `shed`), `top` (NAVD88 metres from the LiDAR: parapet top, or ridge) and, for pitched roofs, `eave` (and `ridge_angle` when the ridge does not follow the long side; a shed roof is high on the left of that direction). `base` stands a part on another part's roof. `style` (`dorm`, `hall`, `plant`, `deck`, `shed`), `floors`, `levels` (deck), `wall`, `trim`, `roof_colour` and `windows` (`width`, `height`, `spacing`) set the look. Outline and heights are measured; the window grid and colours are estimates, so every building object is named `_UNVERIFIED`. A pitched part (gable, hip, shed or mansard, open or not) may take `overhang` (metres): its roof is carried that far past the walls on every edge (wide eaves and rakes, the campus's Mission-style roofs), each roof face in its own plane, so faces of different pitch still meet without a step at the corners (`massing.overhang_roof`); a mansard's flat top and its wells are left as they are. A soffit and fascia close the eaves underneath, in the trim colour or `soffit`. Off unless a part asks for it. `"open": true` makes a canopy: the roof as a slab on a row of posts, no walls (solar canopies over parking, shelters). A part with `height` and no `top` is a plain box that tall. `wells` (`ring`, `floor`, optional `colour`) sink areas into a flat roof's deck or a mansard's flat top: the deck gets a hole on the ring, with walls down to a floor at `floor`. Each ring must lie inside the deck (inside the parapet, or inside the mansard top) and apart from the other wells; the tests check every plan. A roof well is a well; so is a lower roof inside a raised rim of another colour (a wide, shallow well with its own `colour`). A parking deck's ramp bay seen on its roof level is a `shed` part with `style` `deck` (a concrete slab, no levels) standing in a well of the deck's roof whose floor lies at the ramp's low end. A flat part may also take: `parapet` (metres from the parapet top down to the deck; default 0.4, 1.1 on a deck); `ring_roof` (`crest`, `crest_in`, `inner_in`, `colour`, `flat_edges`): a tiled band round the flat roof, from the wall top up to a crest line `crest_in` metres in (a number, or one per edge), a flat crest band to `inner_in`, then a drop to the deck; edges in `flat_edges` (walls shared with another building, or a side another roof covers) get no band, which ends there with a vertical cap; `openings` (`at`, `to` on one wall line, `z0`, `z1`): the wall is cut round each one, for a window wall or door taken over from another model; `no_windows` (edge numbers, in ring order), `windows.levels` (floor heights: one row per floor at `sill` above it, where it is above ground and under the wall top; without `levels` the rows count down from the wall top as before) and `windows.rows` (`[[bottom, top], ...]` in NAVD88 metres, instead of `levels`: rows read off a neighbouring facade, so the two line up; the same ground and wall-top rule). `windows.style` `recessed` cuts each window into the wall as an opening with reveals (`reveal` deep), glass set back (`glass_back`), a frame with a mullion and a transom (`frame` colour) and a sill (`sill_colour`): the main model's CCOM window type. A `ring_roof` may also take `overhang` (a number, or one per edge: the slope carried on past the wall), `caps` (a colour: ridge caps along the crest and hip caps up the convex corners) and `eave_course` (edge numbers: a tile course on the wall top where there is no overhang). An interior slab may give its own `kind` and `colour`.
- **interiors** (an area folder with an `interior.json` and no `footprint.json` owns no ground and has no supersede rules): one entry per floor with `name`, `level`, `floor` and `ceiling` (NAVD88 metres: the bottom and top of its wall runs), `outline` (the building), `inside` (the shell's inner line: nothing is built outside it), `keep_out` (rings of another model's own interior, each with the `objects` it protects and `why`: walls are cut out of them and nothing else may enter them), `walls` (`path`, `thickness`, `openings` `[{"at", "to", "head"}]`, `glazed`), `slabs` and `blocks` (`ring`, `z0`, `z1`; a block's `kind` is `stair` or `lift`), `solids` (piers, `ring`, floor to ceiling), `items` (finishes and fittings: `ring`, `z0`, `z1`, material `kind`, `colour`) and `rooms` (`number`, `at`: label data, no geometry). A slab may name in `under` the keep-outs whose adopted objects stand on it.
- **rough** (in an area's `interior.json`, one entry per building; `rough.py` makes each into `interiors` floors, so the rest of the build and `tools/places/build_rooms.py` treat them like drawn floors): for a public building with no floor plan to draw from. `name` (object prefix), `building` (the OSM name), `osm`, `osm_building` (its `building` tag: housing is refused), `shell` (`main`, `north` or `scripted`: the model whose walls hold it) and `method` (`overlay`: built inside that shell, nothing of it cut; `supersede`: the CCOM method, the shell rebuilt here and taken out of the other tiles), `outline` (the footprint), `wall` (outline to the shell's inner face, default 0.4 m), `levels` (`[{"level", "floor"}]`, NAVD88, rising; a level's own `outline` holds it to an upper block where lower wings stop; `open: true` keeps a level to lining, lobby and cores), `roof` (the top floor's ceiling), `entrances` (`[{"at", "level", "evidence"}]`; each gets a lobby from the nearest point of the nearest corridor straight to it, so a door beyond a corridor's end gets a hall along it), and optionally `spines` (corridor centrelines, `[[[x, y], [x, y]], ...]`; default: the long axis through the centroid), `corridor`, `bay`, `depth`, `cores` (`[{"kind": "stair"|"lift", "at", "size"?, "angle"?}]`; default: a stair at each spine end and a lift across the corridor from the first entrance; a core runs from the lowest floor to the roof, so one outside any level's outline is refused), `toilets` (`[{"level", "ring", "door"?, "evidence"}]`) and `rooms` (`[{"level", "number", "at", "evidence"}]`: room numbers), only where a source places them, `lining: false` (the shell has its own inner faces), `keep_out` (as for drawn floors), `evidence` and `unverified`. Every object is UNVERIFIED. `build_rooms.py` leaves out any interior plan whose objects are not in the published tiles, so `viewer/rooms.json` never names an interior the viewer cannot show; run it again after the export that carries one. Labels show as room numbers (the viewer shows the last word of a label and searches rooms by digits), so give numbers, not names.
- **trees**: a `tree` point may carry `species` (`red_maple`, `sugar_maple`, `pin_oak`, `tulip_poplar`, `american_beech`, `sweetgum`, `white_pine`). `tools/evidence/trees.py` writes a zone of tree points from the LiDAR canopy; do not edit that zone by hand.
- `evidence` names the source (`ortho2020`, `lidar2017`, `lidar2014`, `osm`, a photo file name). `unverified` says in a few words what was guessed.
